Analytical reading is Adler’s third level — a thorough, unhurried reading meant to fully digest a book, aimed at understanding rather than just information. It’s what you do with a book worth reading closely (as opposed to inspectional reading, which is for deciding whether to). Adler breaks it into three stages, each with its own rules:

## Stage 1 — Find out what the book is about

1. Classify the book by kind and subject
2. State what the whole book is about in a sentence or brief paragraph
3. Outline the major parts and how they relate to the whole
4. Define the problem(s) the author is trying to solve

## Stage 2 — Interpret the content (find the author’s meaning) 
5. Come to terms with the author by identifying key words and how they’re used 6. Grasp the author’s leading propositions by dealing with the most important sentences 7. Locate or construct the author’s arguments from sequences of sentences 8. Determine which problems the author solved, and which weren’t

## Stage 3 — Criticize the book fairly (judge it) 
9. Don’t criticize until you’ve fully understood (don’t say “I disagree” until you can say “I understand”) 
10. Don’t disagree disputatiously — argue, don’t quarrel 11. Respect the difference between knowledge and mere opinion, giving reasons for judgments 12. Show where the author is uninformed, misinformed, illogical, or incomplete, before disagreeing

Analytical reading is where Adler’s four questions get their real workout — it’s the level at which you actually extract a book’s structure, meaning, and truth-value rather than just surveying it.
